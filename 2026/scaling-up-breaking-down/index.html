The Multi-Station Kitchen

🥞
Dosa Station
own team
🍚
Idli Station
own team
🧾
Billing Counter
own team

Token Counter
own team
Break the app into small, independent services. Each does one thing. Each can be built, deployed, and scaled independently.

If the idli station is overwhelmed → add one more steamer. You don't rebuild the whole kitchen. If the dosa cook is sick, idlis still flow.

Services talk through well-defined interfaces — like the token number system. You get a token, the kitchen reads it, food arrives.
This is microservices.

Monolith vs Microservices

// Monolith
One Kitchen
✓ One codebase, one deployment
✓ Simple to start with
✗ Hard to scale specific parts
✗ One failure = full outage
// Microservices
Multi-Station
✓ Independent deployments
✓ Scale specific parts easily
✗ Complex to set up
✗ One failure = partial impact
Neither is universally better. It depends on your team size and problem.

Three Design Principles

click to expand →
01
One service, one job
A pod does one thing well. If your service needs a 30-line README to explain what it does — split it.
🍳 The dosa station doesn't do billing.
click to expand →
02
Design for failure
Health checks, readiness probes, graceful shutdowns. If you can't restart without a WhatsApp group — it's not cloud-native.
⚡ Bangalore has power cuts. Rameshwaram has a generator.
click to expand →
03
Keep state out
Stateless services scale beautifully. K8s might restart your pod on a different machine. Don't depend on local memory.
🎫 Rameshwaram doesn't remember your face. You queue fresh every time.

Resilience Patterns

click to expand →
Circuit Breaker
// stop the bleed
Benne dosa counter overwhelmed? Stop sending tokens there. Let it recover. If a downstream service fails 5× in a row, stop calling it for 30 seconds. Let it breathe.
click to expand →
Rate Limiting
// protect the counter
Only 5 people at the counter. The rest wait in line. Protect your services from sudden traffic spikes.
click to expand →
Retries + Backoff
// don't spam
UPI failed? Wait 2s → 4s → 8s. Don't spam it 50 times per second. That just makes things worse for everyone.
Build these in from day one. Not after the first outage.
